Hardik Tankaria

Optimization for AI systems. Autonomous experimentation, agentic architectures, neural-guided search, and the algorithms that make them affordable.

ML Research Engineer, MI-6 Co., Ltd., Tokyo

PhD in Informatics (Mathematical Optimization),
Kyoto University

Kyoto, Japan

I build the decision layer of AI systems: the part that works out what is worth computing, measuring, or asking next, and then proves it was worth it.

That question looks different depending on where you stand. In a self-driving laboratory it is which experiment should the instrument run next, and can we afford it. In computer-aided chemistry it is which of ten thousand candidate reactions deserves the next expansion. In a training loop it is how much curvature information is worth paying for. They are the same problem, and I work on all three.

What I am building now

I am an ML research engineer at MI-6 Co., Ltd. in Tokyo, where I own three concurrent R&D lines.

Autonomous experimentation. A closed-loop Bayesian optimization system for self-driving labs, built so the budget is structural rather than an afterthought: every proposed experiment is priced by a fitted cost model, charged against a ledger, and refused if the remaining budget cannot cover it. Twelve proposal policies, three interchangeable acquisition optimizers, architecture decision records, and a one-command reproducibility check that re-derives every committed figure from committed data. CNN encoders turn instrument images into surrogate inputs, so the loop learns from what the instrument actually sees rather than from a scalar proxy.

Agentic architecture. A packaged, versioned sub-agent that publishes a data contract to a separate optimization engine. Determinism is enforced as a test, the module boundary is checked at process level, and continuous integration gates its own published result counts so they cannot drift. On top of it I am architecting an agentic LLM framework with a knowledge ontology that makes cost-aware experimental decisions, choosing adaptively between a cheap observation and an expensive full characterisation inside a multi-fidelity loop.

Neural-guided search. Monte Carlo Tree Search and bidirectional A* over AND/OR graphs for synthesis planning, with a learned cost-to-go heuristic that cuts exploration at unchanged per-step compute. First-author conference abstract. See the projects →

Where it comes from

My PhD at Kyoto University was on making second-order information affordable at scale, and I pushed every method through to an application rather than stopping at the theorem. A regularized L-BFGS method in Computational Optimization and Applications. A Barzilai-Borwein variant of SVRG in JIMO. The regularized Nyström curvature line, which reduces per-iteration cost from \(O(nd^2 + d^3)\) to \(O(ndm + dm^2)\) with a sketch of roughly 5 to 20 percent of the feature dimension, and keeps the step a descent direction even when the Hessian is indefinite.

Those methods went into brain-MRI tumour classification with transfer learning, where data is scarce and getting more out of each gradient actually matters; into federated learning on edge devices, where the Hessian for a ResNet-50 does not fit on the client and a sketch takes the per-round cost from hours to minutes; and onto high-dimensional classification benchmarks and the CUTEst collection. How I work

Experiments designed before they are run. Paired statistical tests rather than single-run deltas. A stated detection floor beside every comparison. Negative results reported with their mechanism rather than dropped. Most of the levers you try do not work, and knowing which ones did, and why, is the job.

Day to day: Python, PyTorch, BoTorch and GPyTorch, NumPy and SciPy, pytest and CI, Git, MATLAB, RDKit. Things I have shipped: packaged libraries with versioned contracts, benchmark harnesses with thousands of tests, reproducibility checks that fail the build when a figure stops following from its data.

Currently interested in research scientist and research engineer roles where optimization and decision-making are the substance of the work: AI for science, autonomous experimentation, industrial R&D, efficient training, and scientific computing.

selected publications

  1. COAP
    A regularized limited memory BFGS method for large-scale unconstrained optimization and its efficient implementations
    Hardik Tankaria, Shinji Sugimoto, and Nobuo Yamashita
    Computational Optimization and Applications, 2022
    The limited memory BFGS (L-BFGS) method is one of the popular methods for solving large-scale unconstrained optimization. Since the standard L-BFGS method uses a line search to guarantee its global convergence, it sometimes requires a large number of function evaluations. To overcome the difficulty, we propose a new L-BFGS with a certain regularization technique. We show its global convergence under the usual assumptions. In order to make the method more robust and efficient, we also extend it with several techniques such as the nonmonotone technique and simultaneous use of the Wolfe line search. Finally, we present some numerical results for test problems in CUTEst, which show that the proposed method is robust in terms of solving more problems.

  2. JIMO
    A stochastic variance reduced gradient using Barzilai-Borwein techniques as second order information
    Hardik Tankaria and Nobuo Yamashita
    Journal of Industrial and Management Optimization, 2024
    In this paper, we consider to improve the stochastic variance reduced gradient (SVRG) method via incorporating the curvature information of the objective function. We propose to reduce the variance of stochastic gradients using the computationally efficient Barzilai-Borwein (BB) method by incorporating it into the SVRG. We also incorporate a BB-step size as its variant. We prove its linear convergence theorem that works not only for the proposed method but also for the other existing variants of SVRG with second-order information. We conduct the numerical experiments on the benchmark datasets and show that the proposed method with constant step size performs better than the existing variance reduced methods for some test problems.

  3. IJCNN
    NysReg-Gradient: Regularized Nyström-Gradient for Large-Scale Unconstrained Optimization and its Application
    Hardik Tankaria, Makoto Yamada, and Dinesh Singh
    In IEEE International Joint Conference on Neural Networks (IJCNN), IEEE World Congress on Computational Intelligence (WCCI), 2026
    We develop a regularized Nystrom method for solving unconstrained optimization problems with high-dimensional feature spaces. The method leverages actual Hessian information through a regularized Nystrom approximation, integrating the approximated Hessian with gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent. We establish global convergence and analyse the global complexity. Numerical experiments show that it notably outperforms randomized subspace Newton and the approximation of Newton-sketch, showing considerable advancement in optimization with high-dimensional feature spaces.
